 How thecommunity

 The community lives in the Discord: daily
 calls, channels per project, and people you can just ask. This page
 is the map — where to say hi, what the server is like, who to
 ask about anything, and the one rule everything runs on.

 Join the Discord

 When you are in: say hi in #introductions.

The front door

## One hello, a human answers

 The most important step is not a form or a download. Post one hello
 in #introductions — a human will answer. That
 is how this community works: guides and agents come second; people
 come first.

## Do it when you are ready

 For some that is hour one. For some it is week three, after
 quietly watching a few calls. Both are on time.

## Answering is the next step

 The person who answers your first hello was a newcomer once.
 Answering the next hello is one of the first contributions
 available to you — and the one that grows this place
 fastest.

## Ask about what pulls you

 A project you are carrying, plain curiosity, the philosophy, or
 the people — name yours in your introduction. A named why
 is an anchor.

## Islands, not an org chart

 There is no hierarchy here — the community’s one rule is
 sovereignty. These are the areas of the community and the people who
 tend them. It answers one question: who do I ask about X?
 Discord roles (Custodian, R-Moderator, Community Leader, Pioneer-Tech)
 describe involvement, not rank. Ownership works the same way: no
 shareholders, no founder’s seat. The people who tend the islands
 and build in the channels are the people this community belongs to.

For the quiet ones

## You can attend without performing

 Every Discord Stage event — Resonant Academy, AI Stack, Friday
 Automation — has listener/speaker separation built in. You can
 attend purely as audience, and nobody can put you on the spot.

## Camera optional

 Camera off, mic off, just listening is a full way to attend
 anything here. Speak whenever you are ready — including
 never.

## Watching counts

 Watching calls is contribution. Using someone’s tool and
 telling them what broke is contribution. The audience is the
 market, not a holding pen.

## Not everyone is made for the stage

 The band is only one of the roles. So is the sound tech, the
 light design, the manager. Test someone’s tool. Fix a
 wrong meeting time. Answer a question in a channel.

The one rule

## Anti-Capture

 You are sovereign. Nobody prescribes what you build — and you
 never capture anyone else’s sovereignty. That is the Law of
 Anti-Capture, and it is the ethical floor for everything here,
 including the AI tools the community ships. And what you co-build,
 you co-own: the community is held by its contributors, not by anyone
 who bought in.
